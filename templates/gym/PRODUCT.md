## Users
(inferred)

- **Prospective members comparing local gyms.** Mostly on a phone, often arriving from a map listing or a "gym near me" search, sometimes from a friend's recommendation or a social post. They are deciding whether this gym is worth a visit: what it costs, what classes run, whether it feels like their kind of place, and how easy it is to try before committing. Their job on the site is to get enough confidence to start a free trial or book a first class.
- **Current members checking the schedule.** Returning visitors, almost always mobile, who want today's or this week's class times, who is teaching, and whether a class is full. Their job is to find a class and book it in seconds; everything else is in the way.
- **Secondary: parents, corporate-wellness buyers and personal-training clients** who need hours, location, facilities and a way to ask a question.

## Product Purpose
(inferred)

The site exists to turn local interest into free-trial bookings and memberships, and to keep current members engaged by making the class schedule the fastest place to book. Success looks like: free-trial sign-ups, membership joins, class bookings, and phone calls or directions requests from the location page. A secondary success measure is reduced front-desk load — fewer "what time is spin?" calls.

## Operating Context
- **Arrival:** local search and map listings (name, hours, reviews, photos), word of mouth, social video, flyers and signage at the physical location. Most first visits are mobile and quick.
- **Moments of use:** researched (comparing memberships on a weekend evening) and urgent (a member on the way to the gym checking whether the 6pm class is on). The schedule must load fast on poor mobile data.
- **Offline touchpoints the site must support:** tap-to-call phone number, tap-to-navigate address, opening hours by day (including public-holiday notes), walk-in trial availability, and a front desk that may complete the sign-up in person. The site often starts a conversation the staff finish.
- **Seasonality:** January and September spikes; the site should support time-limited offers without redesign.

## Capabilities and Constraints
- **Required pages/sections:** home; memberships (tiers with placeholder prices and what is included); classes (types, intensity, duration) and the weekly schedule; trainers (photo, specialities, certifications); facilities (equipment zones, changing rooms, parking); location and hours; FAQ; free-trial booking; contact; legal (terms, privacy, waiver and health disclaimer).
- **Primary CTAs:** "Start free trial" (persistent on every page), "Join" (on membership tiers). **Secondary:** "Book a class" (on schedule rows and class cards), "Get directions", "Call us".
- **Forms/flows:** free-trial request (name, email, phone, preferred start date, goal), class booking (members), contact/enquiry, newsletter. Membership purchase and member login are typically handled by the gym's management system and are **undecided** here.
- **Must-have information:** opening hours, address and map, membership options and pricing (placeholders until supplied), class schedule, cancellation and freeze policy summary, trial terms, age limits, parking and accessibility details.
- **Integrations typically needed:** class scheduling and booking system (**undecided** — the schedule component is designed natively and can be fed by an API or replaced by a restyled embed), payment/membership platform, maps, analytics, email marketing.
- **Legal/compliance:** a visible health disclaimer ("consult a doctor before starting a new exercise programme") and a note that all members complete a health questionnaire and liability waiver before the first session; clear trial and cancellation terms; privacy notice for form data; cookie consent where required; photo consent for any member imagery.
- **Explicitly undecided:** pricing, schedule integration, online joining, member portal, multiple locations.

## Brand Commitments
The gym's name, logo, wordmark and voice are placeholders to be supplied by the owner. Inferred voice guidance for the industry: direct, energetic, encouraging without being aggressive; short sentences; speak to beginners as warmly as to experienced athletes; avoid body-shaming language and before/after framing; be concrete about what a visitor will experience in their first session.

## Evidence on Hand
No real testimonials, member counts, class statistics, prices, trainer biographies, certifications, photographs, logos or awards exist in this template, and none may be fabricated. All stat numerals, prices and quotes are visibly placeholders. The owner must supply: real membership tiers and prices; the actual class list and schedule; trainer names, photos, qualifications and consent; facility photography; opening hours and address; any accreditations; the waiver and health questionnaire text; and any testimonials with the member's permission.

## Product Principles
1. **The schedule is the product.** For members it is the whole site; it must be fast, native, mobile-first and bookable in two taps.
2. **One tap to try.** "Start free trial" is never more than one tap away on any page, on any device.
3. **Show the real place.** Real facility, real trainers, real class names — no stock imagery that implies a gym that does not exist.
4. **Honest numbers or none.** Stats, prices and results are real and current or they are absent.
5. **Welcome the beginner.** Every page should make a nervous first-timer feel they belong, without diluting the intensity that keeps regulars.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
- WCAG 2.2 AA target; dark theme with body text ≥ 4.5:1 and dark text on the volt CTA.
- Mobile-first with large touch targets (≥ 44px) for one-handed use on the move; sticky trial CTA respects safe-area insets.
- Schedule table must be navigable by screen reader (proper table semantics or an accessible list), and printable in black on white.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`; no autoplaying video with sound.
- Plain-language descriptions of class intensity and beginner-friendliness; note step-free access, accessible changing facilities and adaptive equipment where available.
- Consider multilingual content where the local community needs it; avoid gendered assumptions in copy and imagery.
