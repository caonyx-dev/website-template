## Users

(inferred)

- **Leisure travellers planning a trip.** In research mode over days or weeks, often on a desktop or tablet in the evening and on a phone during the day. They compare destinations, read itineraries day by day, check what is included and excluded, look for a realistic "from" price, and want to understand how booking, deposits, and cancellation work before they talk to anyone. They are deciding whether this operator is credible and whether the trip fits them.
- **Group organisers.** A family, a club, or a company arranging travel for several people. They need to know the operator can handle group sizes, tailor an itinerary, and give a single point of contact.
- **Secondary:** returning clients looking for a new trip, people who have been referred and want to verify licensing and financial protection, and travellers checking practical FAQs after booking.

## Product Purpose

(inferred) The site exists to earn enough trust and clarity that a traveller sends an enquiry or requests a tailored itinerary. It is the operator's showroom and its credentials page in one: it must make destinations desirable, make the process understandable, and make the operator's legitimacy verifiable. Success looks like: qualified enquiries (destination, dates, travellers, budget band) and itinerary requests, fewer pre-sales calls that only ask about inclusions or cancellation, and no enquiry lost because licensing or pricing was unclear.

## Operating Context

- **Arrival paths:** search for destinations and trip types, referrals from past clients, social media and travel content, review and listing sites, and offline advertising. Many visits land on a destination or package page rather than the home page.
- **Devices:** desktop and mobile in roughly equal measure; long itinerary reading happens on larger screens, quick checks and sharing happen on phones.
- **Moments of use:** researched and comparative over multiple sessions; rarely urgent, except post-booking questions.
- **Offline touchpoints the site must support:** a phone number and office hours, email, an office address if there is one, printable or PDF itineraries, and the operator's licence / bonding numbers as they appear on documents.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- **Required pages / sections:** home; destinations (region index and destination pages); packages (listing and package detail with itinerary, inclusions, exclusions, departures, pricing); tailor-made / enquiry; how booking works; FAQs (booking, deposits, payment, cancellation, insurance, passports and visas); about and team; licensing and financial protection; contact; legal (booking conditions, privacy).
- **Primary calls to action:** "Enquire now", "Request itinerary". Present in the header on every page and in a sticky enquiry card on package pages.
- **Secondary calls to action:** "View itinerary", "Download PDF", "Plan a tailor-made trip", "Call us".
- **Flows:** enquiry bar (where / when / travellers) feeding an enquiry form (name, email, phone, destination, travel dates, travellers, budget band, message); package enquiry pre-filled from the package page; tailor-made request form; FAQ accordion.
- **Must-have information:** destination overviews; package itineraries day by day; inclusions and exclusions; departure months or dates; "from" prices as placeholders with a per-person basis note; the booking process and payment schedule; cancellation and amendment policy; insurance requirement; licensing / bonding scheme and number (ATOL-, IATA-, ABTA-style — placeholder); contact details and hours.
- **Integrations typically needed:** email delivery or CRM for enquiries; optional booking engine or availability feed; map embed; optional currency display.
- **Legal / compliance notes:** package travel regulations in the operator's jurisdiction (financial protection disclosure, key information before booking); accurate price presentation with basis and exclusions; licensing and bonding statements must be real and current; no fabricated reviews or ratings; privacy notice and consent for enquiry data; accessibility statement.
- **Explicitly undecided:** booking engine vs. enquiry-only; real prices and currencies; which licensing / bonding scheme applies; whether departures are fixed-date or on-request; review platform integration.

## Product Principles

1. **Trust before desire.** Licensing, financial protection, and the booking process are first-class content, not footer afterthoughts.
2. **Show the trip, not the brochure.** The day-by-day itinerary and the inclusions list are the product; every package must have both.
3. **Prices are honest or absent.** Every "from" price carries its basis and exclusions; no price is shown without them.
4. **Enquiry is the conversion.** The enquiry must be easy to start from anywhere and must capture enough to quote well on the first reply.
5. **Research-mode friendly.** Pages must be shareable, printable, and comparable; visitors come back several times before they commit.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Target **WCAG 2.2 AA**: body text at or above 4.5:1, white on the deep azure button at or above 4.5:1 (the bright azure is used for icons and large text only), visible focus states, minimum 44px touch targets (52px for the enquiry orb and primary buttons).
- Itineraries are long documents: use real headings per day, landmarks, and skip links; keep the timeline readable when linearised by a screen reader.
- Text over photographs must sit on a gradient or scrim that keeps contrast; never rely on the photo alone.
- Date and traveller inputs must be keyboard operable with typed alternatives to the picker and stepper.
- Likely multilingual and multi-currency audience; plan for language switching and clear currency labelling.
- Note accessibility of the trips themselves (walking grades, mobility access, dietary accommodation) as structured content so travellers with access needs can self-qualify.
