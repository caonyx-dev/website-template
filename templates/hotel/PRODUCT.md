## Users

(inferred)

- **Leisure guests** planning a short stay or a special occasion. They arrive from search, a travel article, a map, or an online travel agency and are comparing the hotel against two or three alternatives. Their job is to see the rooms, understand "from" rates and what is included, check availability for their dates, and judge location and atmosphere from photography.
- **Business guests** booking quickly, often on a phone, who need the room type, rate, transport links, workspace, and cancellation terms with as little reading as possible.
- **Event and meeting planners** evaluating function rooms, capacities, catering, and accommodation blocks; they are on desktop, want documents and a human contact, and decide over weeks.
- **Secondary**: diners and spa visitors who are not staying, and returning guests looking for direct-booking offers.

## Product Purpose

(inferred)

The site exists to convert interest into direct bookings, which carry better margins than third-party channels, and to make the hotel's rooms, dining, spa, and location understandable in a few quiet screens. It also supports event enquiries and restaurant and spa reservations. Success looks like availability checks that lead to direct bookings, event enquiries that reach the sales team with enough detail to quote, and phone calls from guests who prefer to speak to someone.

## Operating Context

- **Arrival**: organic and paid search for "[place] boutique hotel", online travel agencies and metasearch linking to the direct site, map listings, travel press, social imagery, and repeat guests. Many visitors land on a room or offer page rather than the homepage.
- **Devices**: mobile-heavy for leisure and business guests checking rates and availability; desktop for planners and for guests completing longer bookings.
- **Moments of use**: researched and comparative for leisure guests; urgent for same-week business travel; deliberate and document-heavy for events.
- **Offline touchpoints the site must support**: phone reservations and concierge calls, email for events and group bookings, arrival directions and parking, restaurant and spa bookings made by phone, and printed confirmations.
- **Content cadence**: rates and availability change constantly and must come from the booking engine; rooms, dining, spa, and location content change rarely; offers change seasonally.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- **Required pages/sections**: homepage with hero and pinned booking widget; rooms and suites index and room detail; offers and packages; dining (restaurant, bar, breakfast); spa and amenities; gallery; location and transport; events and meetings; about; contact; policies; accessibility.
- **Primary calls to action**: "Check availability" (pinned widget, every page), "Book direct" (hero, room detail, offers), "Call" (nav and mobile bar).
- **Secondary calls to action**: view room, view menus, enquire about an event, reserve a table or treatment, join the newsletter, get directions.
- **Forms and flows**: booking widget with check-in, check-out, guests, and promo code handing off to a booking engine; events enquiry (date, attendees, room type, catering, accommodation, contact); restaurant and spa enquiry; newsletter; general contact. All forms need confirmation states and a clear "what happens next" note.
- **Must-have information**: address and transport options; phone and email; check-in and check-out times (placeholder); cancellation and deposit policy (placeholder); room sizes, bed types, occupancy, and views; "from" rates per room and offer as placeholders; dining hours (placeholder); spa and amenity list; accessible room details; parking; pet and child policies as applicable.
- **Integrations typically needed (undecided)**: booking engine for availability and rates; channel manager; table reservation and spa booking systems; map provider; form delivery to the sales team; analytics; optional gift vouchers. The booking engine is explicitly undecided.
- **Legal and compliance**: accurate rate display including taxes and fees per local rules; cancellation terms shown before booking; data privacy and cookie notices; accessibility statement; licensing for the restaurant and bar where required; no misleading claims about ratings or awards.
- **Explicit constraints**: no fabricated reviews, star ratings, awards, or rates. Every rate is a "from —" placeholder and review modules stay empty until genuine, attributed feedback exists.

## Brand Commitments

The hotel name, logo, wordmark, and voice are placeholders to be supplied by the owner. Inferred voice guidance for the industry: quiet, assured, and specific; describe rooms by what a guest will experience (light, view, bed, bath) rather than adjectives; state policies plainly; address the guest directly and warmly; never pressure with countdowns or scarcity claims.

## Evidence on Hand

No real rooms, rates, reviews, star ratings, awards, menus, photographs, logos, licences, or guest data exist in this template, and none may be fabricated. The owner must supply: room inventory with sizes, beds, occupancy, and views; rate structure and booking engine details; offer terms; menus and dining hours; spa treatment list and hours; function-room capacities and floor plans; check-in, check-out, cancellation, pet, child, and parking policies; accessible-room specifications; photography of rooms, public spaces, dining, spa, and surroundings; any genuine guest feedback with permission to publish; licensing and accessibility statements.

## Product Principles

1. **Availability is the first answer.** Dates and guests are reachable on every page, and "Check availability" is never more than one tap away.
2. **Show the room, state the rate, explain the terms.** Photography, a clear "from" rate, and visible cancellation terms do more for direct bookings than persuasion.
3. **Quiet beats loud.** No urgency banners, countdowns, or invented scarcity; the formality of the design is the sales argument.
4. **Honest evidence only.** Ratings, reviews, and awards appear only when real and attributable; placeholders are visibly placeholders.
5. **Direct is worth it.** The site must make the benefits of booking direct explicit and keep parity or better with third-party channels.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Target WCAG 2.2 AA; the design system already requires 4.5:1 for body copy and button labels, keeps gold off ivory as text, and limits weight-300 body copy to sizes where it stays legible.
- The booking widget and date pickers must be fully keyboard-operable with labelled fields and clear error messages; the mobile sheet must trap focus correctly.
- Hero crossfades and gallery fades must respect `prefers-reduced-motion` and offer a pause control.
- Accessible rooms need their own clear specification (door widths, roll-in showers, visual alarms, distance to lift) and must be bookable through the same flow, not only by phone.
- Photographs need meaningful alt text; menus and policies must be real text, not images or PDFs alone.
- Consider multilingual visitors (at least the main inbound-traveller languages), currency display, and plain-language policies.
- Transport and location content should cover step-free access, public transport, and taxi drop-off, not only driving directions.
- Business guests and planners benefit from downloadable fact sheets, but the same information must remain on the page.
