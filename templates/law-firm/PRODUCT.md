## Users

(inferred)

- **Individuals with a legal problem.** Someone facing a divorce, a dismissal, a property dispute, an injury claim, an estate matter, or a charge. They are often stressed, researching late at night on a phone, comparing two or three firms, and trying to answer "does this firm handle my kind of problem, can I trust them, and how do I get hold of someone?"
- **Business owners and in-house contacts seeking counsel.** A founder, director, or operations lead looking for commercial, employment, or property advice, usually from a desktop during working hours, evaluating expertise, seniority of the team, and how engagement and fees work.
- **Existing clients and referrers.** People returning to find a phone number, an address, a specific attorney, or to pass a link on to someone they are referring.

## Product Purpose

(inferred)

The website is the firm's front door. It exists to make a worried visitor feel that the firm understands their situation, to show the breadth of practice areas and the people behind them, and to convert interest into a booked consultation or a phone call. Success looks like consultation requests and inbound calls that are relevant to the practice areas offered, a measurable share of those arriving from mobile, and bio pages that referrers can share with confidence.

## Positioning

Placeholder, to be written by the firm: *"The only firm in [region] where [a specific, verifiable claim: for example, every matter in [practice area] is led by a partner, or the firm has handled [type of matter] for [N] years]."* A generic competitor could not truthfully copy this; it must be a fact about the firm, not an adjective.

## Operating Context

- **How the business works.** Partners and associates grouped by practice area; initial contact through a consultation (paid or free, decided per firm) that leads to an engagement letter. Fees may be hourly, fixed, or conditional depending on practice area and jurisdiction.
- **How visitors arrive.** Local search ("[practice area] lawyer near me"), map listings, personal and professional referrals, directory and review profiles, and occasionally social posts or press. Referrals often land directly on an attorney bio.
- **Devices and moments.** Individuals: predominantly mobile, urgent, frequently outside office hours. Businesses: desktop, researched, during the working day. Both expect a phone number within one tap or glance.
- **Offline touchpoints the site must support.** Phone call (click-to-call on mobile), office visit (address, map, parking, accessibility of the building), email, and a consultation booking that results in a human follow-up.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- **Required pages and sections.** Home; one page per practice area; attorney directory and individual bios; results and approach (process, what to expect, fee structure explained in plain language); FAQ; contact with map, hours, and directions; privacy and legal notices.
- **Primary call to action.** "Book a consultation" (form or booking link). **Secondary.** "Call" (visible phone number in nav, hero, sticky mobile bar, and footer).
- **Forms and flows.** Consultation request form (name, contact, practice area, brief description, preferred time); optional third-party scheduling embed; email notification to the office. No document upload on the public site.
- **Must-have information.** Office address(es), hours, phone, email, bar admissions and jurisdictions per attorney, languages spoken, which practice areas are currently accepting matters.
- **Integrations typically needed.** Map embed, scheduling tool, form delivery to email or CRM, analytics with consent, click-to-call tracking.
- **Legal and compliance notes.** A visible statement that submitting the form or contacting the firm does not create an attorney–client relationship and that confidential details should not be sent until engagement is confirmed. Attorney advertising rules vary by jurisdiction (notices such as "Attorney Advertising", prior-results disclaimers, restrictions on words like "specialist" or "expert", and rules on client testimonials); all such copy must be reviewed by the firm for each jurisdiction in which it practises. Privacy notice and cookie consent as required locally.
- **Explicitly undecided.** Whether the initial consultation is free or paid; whether online booking is live or form-only; number of offices; which practice areas are offered; whether testimonials are permitted in the firm's jurisdiction.

## Brand Commitments

The firm name, logo, and voice are placeholders to be supplied. Inferred voice guidance for the industry: calm, plain-spoken, and authoritative; sentences that explain rather than impress; no jargon without a one-line explanation; no hype, superlatives, or guarantees of outcome; first person plural ("we") for the firm and direct second person ("you") for the visitor.

## Evidence on Hand

None. This template contains no real testimonials, case results, client names, photographs, logos, bar numbers, fee figures, or statistics, and none may be fabricated. The owner must supply: firm name and logo; practice-area list and descriptions; attorney names, titles, bios, admissions, and monochrome portraits; office addresses, hours, and phone numbers; fee and consultation policy; any permitted testimonials or results with jurisdiction-compliant disclaimers; and all legal notices.

## Product Principles

1. **The phone number is a feature.** Every page, every width, one tap away.
2. **Answer the visitor's question before asking for anything.** Practice-area pages explain the problem and the process before the consultation form.
3. **Show the people.** Bios with real credentials outperform generic claims; the attorney page is a destination, not a footnote.
4. **Say less, more carefully.** Legal copy is reviewed per jurisdiction; nothing promises an outcome.
5. **Calm under stress.** Clear hierarchy, generous reading sizes, and no interruptions for a visitor who is already anxious.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Target WCAG 2.2 AA throughout; body text at 16px minimum and contrast of 4.5:1 or better, including on the ivory canvas.
- Mobile-first for individuals in urgent situations; large touch targets, click-to-call, and a sticky action bar.
- Older clients and clients with low vision are common in estates and injury work: respect user font scaling, avoid light-weight type, and keep forms short with visible labels.
- Screen-reader support for the FAQ accordion, the attorney directory, and form errors; keyboard-operable navigation and booking.
- Multilingual readiness: list languages spoken per attorney; structure pages so a translated version can be added per practice area.
- Plain-language summaries at the top of each practice-area page for visitors with low legal literacy.
