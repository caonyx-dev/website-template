## Users
(inferred)

- **Marketing leads at SMEs and startups evaluating an agency.** A head of marketing, founder or growth lead with a budget and a problem (pipeline, launch, rebrand, paid efficiency). They arrive with a shortlist, usually on desktop during work hours, and are comparing three to five agencies on evidence: who have you done this for, what happened, how do you work, what will it cost, and will I like working with you. Their job on the site is to decide whether to book a call.
- **Job applicants.** Strategists, designers, writers and account managers checking whether the agency is somewhere they would want to work: the people, the point of view, the kind of clients, and open roles.
- **Secondary: existing clients and partners** looking for a contact, and press looking for a credentials summary.

## Product Purpose
(inferred)

The site exists to convert qualified leads into strategy calls by demonstrating opinion, process and proof, and to attract talent by showing the people and the culture. Success looks like: booked strategy calls from qualified companies, inbound briefs, case-study views that lead to contact, and applications for open roles. A secondary goal is to reduce unqualified enquiries by being clear about who the agency is for and how it prices.

## Positioning
Placeholder, to be written by the owner: the specific claim a generic agency site could not truthfully copy — a sector the agency knows better than anyone, a method with a name, a pricing model competitors avoid, or a stance on what it will not do. Until supplied, the template uses neutral copy and no comparative claims.

## Operating Context
- **Arrival:** referrals and word of mouth first; then search (agency + sector or service), LinkedIn and social posts by the team, speaking slots, award listings, and links from case studies shared by clients.
- **Devices and moments:** researched, mostly desktop, often in multiple visits over days with a decision made in a meeting; mobile visits come from social links and should still make the work and the call CTA easy to reach.
- **Offline touchpoints the site must support:** a booking link for a strategy call (calendar tool **undecided**), a direct email and phone number, a downloadable credentials deck (optional), and a physical office address if the agency has one.
- **Sales cycle:** weeks, not minutes; the site must support return visits (clear navigation to work, process and pricing approach) rather than a single-session conversion.

## Capabilities and Constraints
- **Required pages/sections:** home; services (what the agency does and does not do); case studies (index with filters and individual case pages: context, approach, outcome, caveat); process (numbered steps with what the client gets at each); team (people, roles, short bios); pricing approach (retainer, project, performance — explained in prose, no figures); contact / book a strategy call; careers (open roles, optional); legal (privacy, cookies).
- **Primary CTAs:** "Book a strategy call" (persistent in nav and at the end of every page). **Secondary:** "See work", "Read the case study", "Meet the team", "See open roles".
- **Forms/flows:** strategy-call request (name, company, role, email, budget range, what you are trying to grow), general contact, job application link. Calendar booking integration is **undecided**.
- **Must-have information:** services with scope, a real case-study set with results stated honestly, the process and typical timelines, team and leadership, how the agency prices, office location (if any), contact details.
- **Integrations typically needed:** calendar booking, CRM/form handling, analytics, applicant tracking (optional), newsletter.
- **Legal/compliance:** results and client names in case studies must be real, accurate and used with client permission; testimonials attributed with consent; no misleading performance claims; privacy notice for form data; cookie consent where required; disclosure of any affiliate or partner relationships.
- **Explicitly undecided:** blog/insights section, careers page, calendar tool, whether to publish indicative price ranges, number of case studies at launch.

## Brand Commitments
The agency's name, logo, wordmark and voice are placeholders to be supplied by the owner. Inferred voice guidance for the industry: opinionated and specific; short declarative sentences; say what you will not do; prefer plain language over marketing jargon; be honest about what did not work in a case study; speak to the client's business outcome before the creative output.

## Evidence on Hand
No real case studies, client names, logos, results, testimonials, team members, photographs, awards or prices exist in this template, and none may be fabricated. Every results numeral ships as an obvious placeholder ("00%") and every case-study card as a placeholder entry. The owner must supply: real case studies with client permission and verifiable figures; client logos with permission; team names, roles, photos and bios; the agency's actual process and timelines; the pricing approach; any awards and press; testimonials with named, consenting sources.

## Product Principles
1. **Proof before promise.** Work and outcomes come first on every path; claims without a case behind them are cut.
2. **Say the caveat.** Case studies include what did not work; honesty is the differentiator in a category full of inflated numbers.
3. **Have an opinion.** The manifesto and the services page state what the agency believes and refuses; a site that agrees with everyone converts no one.
4. **Make the call easy and qualified.** One clear CTA, a short form, and enough information about pricing approach and fit that the right people book and the wrong people self-select out.
5. **Show the people.** Talent and clients both buy the team; real faces and real roles, no stock.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
- WCAG 2.2 AA target; ivory canvas with body text ≥ 4.5:1; violet CTA with ivory text ≥ 4.5:1; highlighter underlines must not be the only link indicator (keep colour + underline).
- Keyboard-navigable case-study filters and sidebar; visible focus rings; offset-shadow press animation must not move the hit target.
- Results numerals carry text captions so screen readers get the metric and its context.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`; no autoplaying video with sound.
- Plain-language explanation of services and pricing approach for non-specialist founders; avoid acronyms without expansion.
- Careers content should state flexible-working and inclusion commitments where real; job posts must be accessible documents, not images.
