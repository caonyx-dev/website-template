## Users

(inferred)

- **Small-business owners.** Founders and managers of companies, partnerships, and sole traders who need bookkeeping, payroll, year-end accounts, tax filings, and occasionally advisory on cash flow, structure, or growth. They are time-poor, comparing two or three practices, and want to know what is included, roughly what it costs, and how onboarding works.
- **Individuals.** People with a tax return to file, a rental property, investment income, self-employment, or a life event (inheritance, relocation, retirement) that makes their tax situation complicated. They are often researching near a filing deadline, on a phone or laptop in the evening.
- **Existing clients.** Returning to find the client-portal link, upload a document securely, check a deadline, or contact their accountant.

## Product Purpose

(inferred)

The website is the practice's front door and its reassurance layer. It exists to explain the services clearly, to show that the team is qualified and the process is organised, and to convert interest into a quote request or a booked call. For existing clients it routes them to the portal quickly. Success looks like qualified quote requests and booked discovery calls, a low volume of "what do you charge?" phone enquiries (because the pricing approach is explained), and portal sign-ins that start from the site without friction.

## Positioning

Placeholder, to be written by the practice: *"The only [region] practice that [a specific, verifiable claim: for example, guarantees a named accountant for every client, publishes fixed-fee packages for small businesses, or turns around year-end accounts within N weeks]."* A generic competitor could not truthfully copy this; it must be a fact about the practice, not an adjective.

## Operating Context

- **How the business works.** Qualified accountants and advisers organised by service line (tax, bookkeeping and payroll, advisory). Engagement begins with a discovery call, a proposal or quote, an engagement letter, then onboarding through a client portal where documents are exchanged. Work is cyclical around filing deadlines.
- **How visitors arrive.** Local search ("accountant for small business [city]"), referrals from clients, banks, lawyers, and business networks, map listings, review profiles, and occasionally professional-body directories. Existing clients arrive from bookmarks and email links.
- **Devices and moments.** Business owners: desktop during the working day, researched over days. Individuals: often mobile, in the weeks before a deadline, urgent. Existing clients: either device, looking for the portal.
- **Offline touchpoints the site must support.** Phone call, booked video or in-person call, email, office visit (address, hours, parking), and secure document exchange through the portal rather than email.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- **Required pages and sections.** Home; services (one page per service line); pricing approach (how fees work, fixed-fee packages or hourly, what is included); process (discovery call to ongoing work); team with credentials; resources and deadlines (filing calendar, guides); client portal entry; contact; privacy and legal notices.
- **Primary call to action.** "Request a quote" (form). **Secondary.** "Book a call" (scheduling link or phone).
- **Forms and flows.** Quote request form (name, contact, business type, turnover band, services needed, current software); discovery-call booking; newsletter or deadline-reminder sign-up. The public site must not accept documents; all uploads go through the secure client portal.
- **Must-have information.** Services and inclusions, pricing approach, office address and hours, phone and email, team qualifications and professional-body membership (licence or registration numbers as placeholders until supplied), portal link, upcoming filing deadlines with a jurisdiction note.
- **Integrations typically needed.** Client-portal link (third-party practice-management product), scheduling tool, form delivery to email or CRM, map embed, analytics with consent, optionally accounting-software partner badges.
- **Legal and compliance notes.** A visible statement that nothing on the site is personalised financial, tax, or investment advice and that figures are illustrative; professional-body registration and any required regulatory wording; privacy notice covering financial data; a note that filing deadlines vary by jurisdiction and entity type; secure-upload guidance (never send documents by email). Testimonials and results, if used, must be real and permitted by the relevant professional body.
- **Explicitly undecided.** Whether pricing is published as fixed-fee packages or "from" figures; which portal product is used; whether the practice is licensed to give regulated investment advice; number of offices; which professional bodies the team belongs to.

## Brand Commitments

The practice name, logo, and voice are placeholders to be supplied. Inferred voice guidance for the industry: precise, plain, and calm; concrete nouns and numbers over adjectives; explain what will happen and when; no jargon without a short definition; no promises of specific savings or outcomes; first person plural ("we") for the practice and direct second person ("you") for the visitor.

## Product Principles

1. **Make the numbers trustworthy.** Every figure is exact, labelled, and clearly placeholder or real; precision is the brand.
2. **Explain the engagement before asking for it.** Pricing approach and process pages answer "what will this cost and what happens next" before the quote form.
3. **Route existing clients fast.** The portal link is always one click away and never buried in marketing.
4. **Security is visible.** Document exchange goes through the portal, and the site says so plainly.
5. **Deadline-aware.** Resources and reminders earn return visits and reduce last-minute panic.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Target WCAG 2.2 AA throughout; body text at 15px minimum and contrast of 4.5:1 or better, including positive-number green text (use the deep green for text).
- Tables of deadlines and fees must be real HTML tables with headers, readable by screen readers, and must collapse to lists on mobile without losing meaning.
- Numbers never carry meaning by colour alone: positive and negative figures also use a sign or label.
- Mobile-first for individuals near a deadline; large touch targets, click-to-call, and stacked buttons in the hero.
- Forms with visible labels, inline errors, and no time limits; respect user font scaling.
- Plain-language explanations for visitors with low financial literacy; glossary terms defined inline on resource pages.
- Multilingual readiness: structure service pages so translated versions can be added; list languages spoken per team member.
