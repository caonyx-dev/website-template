## Users

(inferred)

- **Local customers on a phone** — a resident who needs a plumber, a haircut, a cleaner or a repair soon, often today or this week. They found the business on a map listing or a search, want to know "do you do this, roughly what does it cost, are you open, can I call or book now", and decide in under a minute.
- **Returning customers** — checking hours, rebooking, or finding the phone number.
- **Secondary**: local businesses looking for a commercial version of the service; people checking reviews before referring a friend.

## Product Purpose

(inferred) The site exists to turn local intent into a call, a booking or a quote request. It lists services with starting-price placeholders, shows hours and service area, offers social proof (reviews placeholder), answers common questions, and keeps "Call" and "Book" one tap away on mobile. Success looks like phone calls, booking requests and quote requests from people in the service area, and fewer "are you open?" calls because the hours are clear.

Placeholder, to be written by the owner: "[Business] is the [town] [trade] that [the claim a generic competitor could not truthfully copy — e.g. same-day availability, a specific specialism, transparent pricing, or a long local history]." The template ships with this line marked as a placeholder.

## Operating Context

- Visitors arrive via map listings and the Google Business Profile, local search ("[service] near me"), word of mouth, neighbourhood social groups, review platforms and vehicle or shopfront signage.
- Mobile dominates; many visits are short and urgent. The site must load fast on a weak connection and work one-handed.
- Moments of use: urgent (leak, same-day appointment) and planned (weekly clean, a service next month). Both need hours, phone and booking visible immediately.
- Offline touchpoints the site must support: phone calls, text messages, walk-ins to a physical address, and email.
- The site's name, address, phone, hours and categories must match the Google Business Profile exactly so listings and the site reinforce each other.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- **Required pages / sections**: home (hero with call/book, services, trust line, reviews placeholder, hours, service area, FAQ, contact), services (per service with "from" price placeholders), about (owner / team, short story), reviews placeholder, FAQ, contact / booking (form, hours card, map card, address).
- **Primary CTAs**: "Call" (tel: link), "Book" (form or booking widget), "Get a quote". A sticky mobile bar carries Call + Book.
- **Booking / quote form fields** (undecided, suggested): service, preferred date and time, name, phone, address or postcode, notes; consent checkbox.
- **Must-have information**: hours (including holidays), phone, address, service area (towns or postcodes), starting-price placeholders, payment methods accepted (placeholder), insurance or licence note where relevant (placeholder).
- **Integrations typically needed**: click-to-call, booking widget or calendar (optional), form handling with spam protection, map embed, review feed (optional), local-business structured data, analytics with consent.
- **Legal / compliance**: trade licensing display where applicable, privacy policy, cookie consent if analytics are used, accessibility statement.
- **Explicitly undecided**: whether bookings are online or by phone only, whether prices are published, the exact service list, review source, languages.

## Brand Commitments

- Business name, logo, photos, owner portrait, prices, reviews and voice are placeholders to be supplied by the owner.
- Inferred voice for the industry: warm, plain, local, reassuring; sentence-case; first person plural ("we'll"); short sentences; no jargon; promises only where the owner can keep them.

## Evidence on Hand

No real reviews, ratings, prices, photos, owner or staff details, hours, service area, response-time promises, "since [year]" claims or insurance statements exist in this template, and none may be fabricated. The owner must supply: service list and starting prices, hours, address, phone, service area, photos of work and premises, team details, verified reviews or a review feed, and any licensing or insurance details.

## Product Principles

1. **Answer the four questions first.** What you do, what it roughly costs, when you're open, how to reach you — above the fold on a phone.
2. **One tap to a human.** Call and Book are always visible; forms are short and work with autofill.
3. **Match the map.** The site mirrors the Google Business Profile so listings, reviews and the site never contradict each other.
4. **Local proof.** Reviews, service area and recent work from the neighbourhood matter more than polish.
5. **Easy to keep true.** Hours, prices and services must be trivial to update; stale hours cost customers.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Orange and pastels are never text on white; white on green stays at 15 px / 600 or larger.
- Large pill buttons (48 px) and a sticky call/book bar for one-handed use; the bar hides when a form field is focused.
- Hours and status ("Open now") are conveyed in text, not colour alone; phone numbers are real tel: links.
- Alt text on work photos; the map has a text list of areas served.
- Readable at 200 % zoom; reduced-motion respected for the FAQ and review carousel.
- Older customers and non-native speakers: plain language, generous line height, and room for a second-language toggle where the community needs it.
